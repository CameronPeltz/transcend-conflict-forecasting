The headline change

For the first time: a real escalation inside the radio-covered window

Checked directly against the GDELT-derived label: the week of 2026-04-27 is a real, confirmed positive — DRC's material-conflict share and Goldstein score both moved sharply during the following week (2026-05-04: material-conflict share 0.31 vs. a 0.07–0.19 norm; mean Goldstein −1.42, driven substantially by CAMEO root category 19, "Fight," which alone accounted for 72 of that week's 297 coded events). Every prior round of this analysis had zero positive examples to test against; this round has exactly one. One is still not a statistically powered sample — precision/recall from n=1 would be meaningless rounding — but it is a real, specific, checkable case, used directly in tab 02.

Radio coverage for that specific week is partial: the radio dataset starts May 1, and the week in question (Apr 27 – May 3) only has 3 of 7 days' worth of real coverage (260 clips, vs. 700–3,800+ in later, fully-covered weeks). Worth knowing when reading tab 02's result for this case.
Current best approach

The established ensemble, tested as the baseline

Per this project's own prior findings (results_v2/final-summary-and-case-studies.html), an ensemble of gradient-boosted trees, random forest, and logistic regression is the strongest real approach on fatality/event-coded data of this kind — it topped the original UCDP track (AP 0.871) and is the natural baseline here. (The hypergraph neural network, this project's other headline model, was deliberately not used as the baseline: on this same kind of event-coded track it was the project's weakest family, not its best — see Finding 2 in the earlier results — so using it here would misrepresent "current best approach.")

The precise, complete truth about where the 52.6% improvement actually comes from: almost entirely from sharply better-calibrated predictions on the 13 real quiet weeks (several roughly halved). The one real escalation week is essentially unchanged (0.377 → 0.382) — radio signal neither helped nor hurt that specific call, and neither version crosses 0.5 for it. So: a real, substantial, honestly-earned reduction in false-alarm tendency, and no demonstrated improvement yet in catching the one real escalation available to test against.
Direct answer

Did radio data measurably improve outcomes?

Yes, measurably, for false-alarm reduction — 30 of 30 tested configurations, up to a real 52.6% cut in prediction error. No, not yet demonstrated for catching a real escalation — the only available real positive case was ranked essentially identically with or without radio signal, and missed by both at a standard threshold. Both halves of that sentence are real findings from the same test; reporting only the first would overstate what's been shown.

Recommended next steps

Ranked by what would most directly close the remaining gap

  1. 1

    Get full radio coverage for the week immediately before the escalation (Apr 20–26)

    The escalation-predicting row's own week (Apr 27–May 3) only has 3 of 7 days of real radio coverage, and its lag-1 input (Apr 20–26) has none at all. A complete two-week radio window bracketing this real event is the single most direct way to test whether fuller coverage changes the 0.377→0.382 near-miss.

  2. 2

    Extend collection forward in time to accumulate more real positive examples

    One confirmed escalation is real progress over zero, but still far short of a powered sample. Every additional month of real DRC radio collection is the direct, unglamorous path to a result as statistically credible as Criterion 2's 858-prediction UCDP figure.

  3. 3

    Try a threshold specifically tuned for recall on real escalations, not just calibration

    Every result on this page evaluates raw probability error (MSE). A precision/recall-style operating-threshold analysis, like the one built for Criterion 2, becomes meaningful once there are enough real positive weeks (next steps 1–2) to support it honestly.

  4. 4

    Investigate the "armed-actor + location mentions" feature group specifically

    It was the best or near-best configuration in all three rounds of this analysis now (this round, the prior 6,828-clip round, and directionally in the first). That consistency across three different real data snapshots is itself evidence worth taking seriously into how Phase II's Task 1 ingestion pipeline weights radio-derived features.

  5. 5

    Do not update the formal DARPA proposal with a new precision number yet

    Unchanged recommendation from the prior two rounds, for the same reason: one real positive example still cannot support a defensible precision/recall claim. The 52.6% false-alarm-reduction finding is real and worth describing as progress — the distinction between "reduces false alarms" and "improves detection" needs to stay explicit wherever this is reported.
