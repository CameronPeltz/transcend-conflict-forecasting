The real, honest headline: with more data, the earlier gap between "filter to high-relevance clips first" and "use everything" mostly closed. Before, unfiltered configurations only helped 10 of 15 times — some were worse than the no-radio baseline. Now every single configuration tested, filtered or not, reduced prediction error relative to baseline. The most likely honest explanation: more clips per week means keyword-based aggregate features average out noise better even without pre-filtering, which is exactly the kind of thing more data should do.

Unchanged, and this is the important part

What still hasn't been proven — more data didn't fix this

Zero real escalations occurred anywhere in the radio-covered window, checked directly against the GDELT-derived label across every week from late June through early August. Real DRC event volume actually trended down over this exact period even as conflict-related radio chatter stayed high (40–60% of clips). That means everything in tab 02 is still, honestly, a measure of reduced false-alarm tendency on confirmed-quiet weeks — not evidence the system would catch a real escalation. That distinction doesn't go away with more data; it only goes away with a radio-covered window that actually contains an escalation to test against.

Recommendation unchanged from the first round: this is real, positive, worth reporting progress — and still not a defensible new precision claim for the formal proposal. The May–August expansion in progress (10,000+ clips, 99 days) meaningfully improves the odds of finally covering a real escalation, which is the actual blocker to a rigorous claim, not feature engineering.
